    Always on

    Health and recovery

    Watch every account weekly. When a trigger fires, run the first-week recovery.

    Start a recovery when

    • Weekly active builders fall 30% or more for two weeks in a row
    • No new app reaches production in three weeks
    • The sponsor or champion changes roles or leaves
    • More than a quarter of trained builders have stalled
    • Usage is pacing well below the contract commitment
    • Builders hit their spend limit, which pauses Clark until an admin raises it
    • Publishes keep getting blocked by Code Security findings

    Where to look: Plan & Usage (usage by user, app and day, and published-app views), audit logs, or the Admin MCP.

    First week of a recovery

    • 1Day 1. Pull usage data: who stopped building, which apps lost users, and when it started.
    • 2Days 2–3. Talk to the champion and two or three stalled builders. Ask what got in the way, not whether they like the product.
    • 3Day 3. Name the cause: data access, no clear use case, sponsor change, spend limits, blocked publishes, a product gap, or a team change.
    • 4Day 4. Pick two quick wins, each with a named owner and a date.
    • 5Day 5. Send the sponsor a recovery note with the numbers, the cause and a 30-day plan.
    Always on

    Requests and feedback

    Every customer request is logged once with what they asked for, the outcome behind it, how many customers need it, and whether a workaround exists. Then it goes to one of three places.

    • 1Works today. The strategist shows them how, and adds it to the training material if others will ask too.
    • 2Needs setup or an integration. Goes to the forward deployed engineer's queue, ranked by how much it moves the customer's success plan.
    • 3Product gap. Goes to Product with the business case, the customers asking and the revenue involved. No roadmap promises to the customer.

    Close the loop: tell the customer where the request went and when they will hear back.

    Open questions

    This draft is built from the outside, using public docs. These answers would change it.

    1. How are deals handed over from Sales today, and what context tends to get lost?
    2. Which of these materials already exist, and which would help most to build first?
    3. What do the current usage dashboards track, and what is missing to tell the value story?
    4. What is the most common reason customers stall, and does a Hybrid or Cloud-Prem deployment change that?
    5. How long do Hybrid and Cloud-Prem setups take in practice? This draft assumes about one and two extra weeks.
    6. How is forward deployed engineer time split across accounts, and who decides?
    7. How many existing customers are still mainly on Low-Code, and is moving them to Clark part of this role?
    8. How does the team track usage against each customer's commitment, and what pace counts as healthy?
    9. When does renewal planning start today, and who owns it with the account executive?

    About this draft

    A customer deployment playbook drafted by Sardar Ahmar Wasif for a conversation with the Superblocks team, and built with Claude Code.

    • What it is. One repeatable process for a new customer, from signed contract to renewal, that adapts to each account's setup and readiness.
    • What it is not. An official Superblocks document or product. The console-style layout is an homage to make it easy to picture in use.
    • Data. The SoFi example account uses only SoFi's public information, with sources listed on its overview. Its profile, readiness answers and brief are my assumptions to confirm at handoff, not Superblocks or SoFi data. The weekly sponsor note is a fictional sample.
    • Sources. Product details come from the Superblocks docs, the pricing page and How to Prove AI ROI to Your Board, as of October 2026. Timelines are estimates, listed under Open questions.
